Doctrine, assessed — with the evidence attached

Wardley's own analogy is military: doctrine is the training of your forces — the standard techniques any unit benefits from, drilled regardless of which battle they end up in. You would not send troops into a fight to learn how to communicate. Strategy tells you where to attack; doctrine is why you are capable of attacking anything.

Strategy and mapping are context-specific. Doctrine always applies — so an organisation's ability to handle a hard problem tends to track how well it practises doctrine, before any strategy is chosen.

One distinction matters, and Wardley is explicit about it: these are principles you choose because the evidence says they are broadly useful — not immutable laws. Several writers separate doctrine, meaning chosen and evidence-based good practice, from dogma, meaning belief held past the point of questioning. Rating yourself against forty of them, as this page does, is only useful if you are willing to argue with the rating.

Why this page is here, and what it is assessing. This assessment was built for pki.sgit.ai's registry MVP pack and lived at appendix depth inside it. The subject has not changed — it is still that pack, assessed as if built as specified — but a doctrine assessment belongs on a mapping site, and there is currently no working doctrine assessment tool anywhere in the ecosystem: both doctrine.wardleymaps.com and Stach's are dead, and awesome-wardley-maps still lists them. The obituaries → A worked example plus a public schema is not a tool. It is the closest thing that currently exists, and it costs nothing extra. The evidence links point back at pki.sgit.ai, because the assessment moved and the artefacts it rests on did not.

How they are organised

Roughly forty principles, grouped into six categories and sequenced into four phases of increasing maturity. The phases are the useful part: they say what to fix first. There is no point distributing decision-making before anyone agrees what the words mean.

The one rule that makes a self-assessment worth publishing

Every rating names the artefact it rests on. Not a justification — a link. So a reader who disagrees with a rating has the evidence in front of them and can say so with something specific, rather than forming a general impression that the page is generous to itself.

That discipline is why this page belongs on a site whose first sentence is maps are claims, not pictures. It is the same move: state the claim, attach what it rests on, make disagreeing cheap. Q5 asks the obvious follow-on — no map in the corpus attaches evidence to a single placement, and if this is the right discipline for a doctrine rating it is hard to argue it is the wrong one for a position on the evolution axis.

Honest tensions in the assessment itself

TensionNote
Self-assessedThe party being rated wrote the ratings. This is the weakest kind of assessment there is, and the page says so rather than hoping you do not notice. Mitigated only by naming the artefact behind each one, which turns disagreement into something a reader can act on rather than merely feel.
"No basis yet" is convenientFive doctrines are excluded because there is no organisation. That is true, and it is also the answer that flatters the subject most — treat those five as unearned rather than neutral.
The phase model implies a ladderWardley sequences the phases, and real projects practise bits of Phase III while missing bits of Phase I. This one does exactly that.
Forty is approximateThe canonical list is "roughly forty" and different sources group and name them slightly differently. The categories and phase placements here follow the briefing this was built from; a few placements are ours and are arguable.
Rating a design, not a running systemThe registry is unbuilt. Doctrines about operations and flow are rated against how the project is run, which is a weaker claim than rating a service.
The countThe research brief that commissioned this site describes this assessment as 41 records — 15 strong, 11 partial, 6 weak, 9 no-basis. The artefact holds 40, split 17 / 12 / 6 / 5. The artefact wins; the brief's figures are a summary that drifted. Logged as N1.

The data, as an endpoint

/doctrine/doctrine.json is served as a stable URL. Per record: id, cat, phase, name, what, status, us, evidence — where evidence is a list of [label, url] pairs and status is one of strong (Practised) · partial (Partly) · weak (Not practised) · na (No basis yet). The file carries its own caveat, statuses, categories and phases blocks, so it is self-describing: you do not need this page to read it.

Fork it, replace the us and evidence fields with your own, and you have a doctrine assessment of your own organisation in a format somebody else can diff. That is what filling the gap looks like without building a tool.
