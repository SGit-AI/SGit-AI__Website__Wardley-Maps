What do Wardley maps really map? A settler writes (critique)

Reference & derivative Live critique

#7 of the ten to read first. Read the strongest objection early, before over-committing. The consensus-versus-evolution argument will change how you use the technique — and it is the argument this site's agent guardrail is built on.

What it is

The most substantive published critique of Wardley mapping, and the one that has genuinely stuck — a version of its central objection now appears in the Wikipedia article. Edgar, self-identifying as "a serial settler," raises three objections. First, that organising around pioneers/settlers/town planners is actively harmful, producing turf wars rather than clarity — he cites Orange Group wasting effort arguing about which category the app store belonged to. Second, that the evolution axis implies an inevitable left-to-right progression that reality does not honour: components stall, and components move backwards, and "we're terrible at making predictions. Especially about the future." Third and most damagingly, that "Wardley maps are therefore an artefact of social science, not a life science" — that the x-axis measures level of consensus, not objective evolution, which means the technique is manipulable and, rather than exposing assumptions for challenge, may launder assumptions into facts and delegitimise challenge while still manufacturing consensus.

Limitations

From 2017; does not engage with the doctrine grid or with anything from the last nine years of practice. Blog-length, not rigorous — it is an argument, not a study. No licence, so you can quote briefly but not reproduce.
