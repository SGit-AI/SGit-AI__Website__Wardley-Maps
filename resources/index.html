34 industry resources, verified rather than listed

Not "here are some links". Here is every link, checked on a stated date, with what is actually dead. As of August 2026 the community's own canonical indexes are wrong: five dead things are still listed as live by awesome-wardley-maps, by Wikipedia, and in two cases by Simon Wardley's own resources page.

That is a low bar and a real service. A site that verifies every link and publishes the verification date is immediately more useful than the community's index — so this one does, on a schedule, and publishes the run.

The practice is healthy; the link layer rotted. Map Camp is returning after a two-year gap with a workshop on 12 November 2026 and a full conference in 2027, Mermaid support has made maps native to git, OnlineWardleyMaps got a full rewrite in 2025, and ArcKit is bringing mapping into agentic workflows with real adoption. What died is the directory, not the technique. Which is fixable, and fixing it is a good reason for this page to exist.

Every page below verified 2026-08-23 — status, licence and links checked against the source on that date, and re-checked on a schedule since. The runs →

Three ways in

A flat A–Z is the wrong primary navigation for 34 things when a reader's actual question is "where do I start". Three routes, then the list.

You have never drawn one

Roughly four hours, in this order. The third step is the one people skip and the only one that actually teaches anything.

  1. WardleyMaps.com — Wardley Mapping 101 — fifteen minutes, one sustained worked example.
  2. Crossing the River by Feeling the Stones (talk) — Forty-eight minutes. Free. Watch Wardley reason with a map rather than present one.
  3. OnlineWardleyMaps (OWM) — Now draw one. Map something you already understand deeply — not your company, where you will confuse what you wish were true with what is.
  4. The Wardley Mapping Canvas — Use it as the process for that first map: needs, then chain, then position.
  5. Learn Wardley Mapping (LearnWardleyMapping.com) — The free reference, then the doctrine grid — self-assess against Phase 1 only.
  6. Wardley Maps — Chapters 1–6. Stop there for now; the rest keeps.
  7. What do Wardley maps really map? A settler writes (critique) — Before you evangelise. The strongest objection, from someone sympathetic.
  8. Map Camp — Discord via mapcamp.co.uk — and put 12 November 2026 in the diary.

You work in security

Mapping is the missing half of threat modelling — a claim made on this site's own chronology in March 2022 and never followed up in public. This is the route that follows it up.

  1. WardleyMaps.com — Wardley Mapping 101. Do not skip it because you are senior; the vocabulary is load-bearing.
  2. Using Wardley Mapping for Optimizing Your Threat Modeling Strategy — Xebia's method: map first, mark team boundaries as bias hotspots, ask what can be deleted, then STRIDE only the areas the map flagged.
  3. Wardley mapping in security — The Open Security Summit session where the claim was first made.
  4. Evolution-informed Security Architecture: Using Wardley Mapping (SABSA) — Mario Platt's webinar — mapping against an architecture framework you may already run.
  5. Where the map ends: understanding Wardley maps' limitations (critique) — This one matters most for you. Two of its four limitations are directly disabling in security: components only evolve under competitive pressure — much of internal security faces none — and you cannot map what you cannot see, which is definitionally the threat that gets you.
  6. Learn Wardley Mapping (LearnWardleyMapping.com) — Doctrine Phase 1, which reads as a security maturity model with the serial numbers filed off.
  7. Wardley Maps in Mermaid (wardley-beta) — Put the maps in git so they sit next to the ADRs and get reviewed in pull requests.

You are an agent, or you are building one

The full treatment is /agents/, including the coordinate contract proven by rendering and the epistemic guardrail. The reading list is here.

  1. Wardley Maps in Mermaid (wardley-beta) — Grammar first. Coordinates are [visibility, evolution], not [x, y].
  2. OnlineWardleyMaps (OWM) — The DSL reference — the format everything else targets.
  3. Simon Wardley's map repository — Ground truth: 147 maps, ~4,905 components, ~5,172 links, 22 sectors. Paired OWM/Mermaid representations make an excellent format-translation training pair.
  4. ArcKit (Wardley mapping for AI coding assistants) — Do not reinvent this. MIT, actively maintained, and note its decomposition into wardley.value-chain, wardley.doctrine, wardley.gameplay, wardley.climate rather than one monolithic make-a-map action. Mirror that decomposition.
  5. cli-owm — Deterministic rendering — stdin to SVG through a real OWM parser, so the output is verifiable rather than merely plausible.
  6. What do Wardley maps really map? A settler writes (critique) — Mandatory. The evolution axis plausibly encodes consensus, not fact. An LLM placing components on it is generating consensus-shaped output with no underlying evidence — Edgar's laundering assumptions into facts, at scale and at speed.

If you only look at ten

In this order.

  1. Wardley Maps — The primary source, and the chapter that establishes why before how. The CC BY-SA 4.0 licence on it is what makes the whole ecosystem exist.
  2. Learn Wardley Mapping (LearnWardleyMapping.com) — The pedagogy the book lacks. The free reference alone — especially the four-phase doctrine grid — is the fastest route from confusion to competence.
  3. Crossing the River by Feeling the Stones (talk) — Forty-eight free minutes of Wardley doing the thing. The highest-conversion artefact in the list: people who bounce off the book do not bounce off this.
  4. OnlineWardleyMaps (OWM) — You do not understand mapping until you have drawn one. Also the format standard the whole ecosystem targets, so learning its DSL is not tool lock-in.
  5. awesome-wardley-maps — The community index, and the only major resource under CC0 — so also the only one you can freely reuse as a base. Five of its entries are dead; ours says which.
  6. WardleyMaps.com — Home of Wardley Mapping 101 — the best free fifteen-minute end-to-end worked example, one sustained case study rather than a tour of features.
  7. What do Wardley maps really map? A settler writes (critique) — Read the strongest objection early, before over-committing. The consensus-versus-evolution argument will change how you use the technique — and it is the argument this site's agent guardrail is built on.
  8. Wardley Maps in Mermaid (wardley-beta) — The most consequential recent development: maps in git, in pull requests, in documentation, with no tool at all. See the gallery.
  9. Simon Wardley's map repository — 147 real maps across 22 industries. Twenty of these teach more than two more chapters. ⚠️ Licence contradictory — link, do not mirror.
  10. Where the map ends: understanding Wardley maps' limitations (critique) — The four structural limitations, stated fairly. The thing that stops mapping becoming a religion.
